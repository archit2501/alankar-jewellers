The reverse

The part with no audience.

In Jadau and Polki work the back of a piece is enamelled — opaque green, red and white meenakari fired into gold that nobody but the wearer will ever see.

It is the half of the craft that cannot be sold on sight, which is exactly why it tells you the most about who made the thing.

You should not have to take our word for the side you cannot see.

At the bench

Made slowly. Worn forever.

A jadau setting is not claw work. The stone is bedded into shellac and the gold is worked up around it in thin foil, one stone at a time, until the metal closes on the girdle by itself.

The enamel goes on before any of that, because it has to be fired hot enough that no stone could survive it. Which is to say: the back of the piece is made first.
